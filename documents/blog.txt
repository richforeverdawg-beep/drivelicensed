how-to-spot-a-fake-driver-license|How to Spot a Fake Driver License in Under Five Minutes|Seven physical tests that separate a printed licence from a real one, and the three questions that expose a seller who has never held the document.|A fake licence fails on physics before it fails on data. That is the useful part, because physics is something you can test on a kitchen table in daylight while the seller waits.|Start with the ghost image. Every modern licence carries a faint secondary portrait, usually offset from the main photo and printed in a tone that disappears at a shallow angle. On a colour photocopy it flattens into a grey patch that stays visible at every angle. Tilt the card through forty degrees and watch for the change.|Next is microprint. The signature line, the border or a strip beneath the photo is set in text around one point high. On a real card it resolves into letters under a loupe. On a copy it collapses into a dotted line. A ten pound jeweller loupe settles this in seconds and no seller can talk around it.|Third is the substrate. Polycarbonate cards flex and spring back. Teslin and PVC feel different again but both are dense and slightly warm to the touch. Paper laminated between two sheets is thinner, flexes further and clicks when tapped. Ask the seller to tap the card on video call and listen.|Fourth is the UV layer. Every authority in the developed world prints UV ink, usually a flag, a crest or a repeating pattern across the data field. Under a cheap UV torch it appears sharp and saturated. A print shows nothing, or shows a dull bloom where the paper was coated.|Fifth is the number. Licence numbers are constructed, not random. They encode surname, birth date, issue sequence or a check digit depending on the country. Break the rule and the card is wrong regardless of how good the print is. Look up the format for the issuing state before you look at any photo.|Sixth is the category grid. This is the hardest part of a licence to typeset convincingly, because the rows are fixed, the font is specific and the alignment is tight. Fakes cram categories, use the wrong weight, or leave a row empty that should carry a restriction code.|Seventh is the address. Fakes usually inherit the address from the real card they were copied from, so the postcode will be correct for the wrong street or the state will contradict the header. Compare the postcode against the issuing office area, not against the street.|Now the three questions. Which office issued it, and what is the district code. What is the check digit rule for this state. And what does the card look like under UV. A seller holding real stock answers all three without notes. A reseller pauses on the second and improvises on the third.|What if the seller only has photos?|Then run the tests you can run on photos, which are the number, the category grid and the address consistency, and ask for a UV shot before paying.|Is a perfect fake possible?|Yes, with real stock and the right printer. That is why the number and the register matter more than the print quality once you are past the obvious tells.|How much should a licence cost?|Between 120 and 650 US dollars depending on whether it is a print, correct stock, or a card with a number in a real format.
passport-mrz-check-digits|What a Passport MRZ Check Digit Actually Does|The machine-readable zone is not decoration. It is a checksum, and it catches every hand-edited data page ever made. Here is how the maths works and how to run it.|Two lines of chevrons and capitals at the foot of a passport data page look like bureaucracy. They are a checksum, and they exist so a machine can tell in one pass whether the page above has been altered.|The zone encodes the document type, the issuing state, the holder name, the passport number, the nationality, the birth date, the sex, the expiry date and a personal number. Each of those fields is followed by a single digit computed from its own characters.|The computation is a weighted sum. Letters get values from ten upward, digits keep their own value, and the weights cycle seven three one across the field. The total is divided by ten and the remainder is the check digit. It is arithmetic, not a secret.|This is why an edited date of birth is fatal. Change one digit on the visual page and the corresponding field in the zone no longer matches its check digit. Any reader app flags it immediately, and airline check-in systems read the same numbers.|It is also why a good fake reproduces the zone faithfully. The zone on a competent replica computes correctly because it was copied from a real book or generated from the same data. A correct zone proves consistency, not authenticity.|So the zone tells you two things. If it fails, the document is definitely altered or definitely printed by somebody who did not understand the format. If it passes, the document is internally consistent and you move to the physical layer.|The physical layer is where the real work is. Polycarbonate data pages have a distinct flex and a sealed edge. Holographic overlays shift with the angle. UV print sits under the laminate and is sharp. Intaglio printing on older books has a raised feel along the guilloche.|Run a UV torch along the data page and look for the issuing state pattern. Almost every state prints one. A page with no UV response is a print, whatever the zone says. And check the laminate edge for a cut line, which means the sheet was laid over a page rather than fused into it.|What app reads the zone?|Any free MRZ reader from an app store. They do not authenticate anything, they only validate the checksum, which is still the fastest test available.|Can a fake have a valid zone?|Yes. A valid zone only means the data agrees with itself. Always pair it with a UV and substrate check.|Does a failing zone mean the book is worthless?|It means the page was edited or badly generated. That is enough to renegotiate or walk away.

The register behind it is the USCIS alien registration system.|A residence permit is the fixed term card a country issues to a foreign national it has allowed to stay. It is marketing language, not a document category.|Can I hold both?|In sequence, yes. Fixed term permit first, permanent card later, in most systems. Not at the same time for the same country.

uv-light-checks|UV Light Document Checks Explained|A three pound torch exposes more document fraud than any app. This is what UV ink does on a real document, why copies cannot reproduce it, and how to read the response properly.|Ultraviolet ink is the cheapest serious security feature on any document and the one sellers most often forget. Passport data pages carry a state pattern or flag across the portrait field. Identity cards carry a crest or a repeating motif. Licences carry an authority mark, often a coat of arms, along the bottom edge.|The ink is applied by the issuing authority and sits under the laminate, so it is sharp, saturated and precisely placed. A colour photocopy shows nothing at all, because the copier cannot see it. A digital print on coated stock shows a dull bloom where the coating was applied.|That difference is the tell. Real UV print is crisp at the edges. A print shows a fuzzy patch or a bright smear across the whole card, because the coating was sprayed rather than printed.|Buy a torch that outputs at three hundred and sixty five nanometres and use it in a dark room. At that wavelength the ink fluoresces strongly and paper stays dark. Cheap novelty torches at four hundred nanometres wash everything out.|Work across the card slowly and watch for two things: the pattern itself and the placement. A pattern in the wrong position is as good as no pattern, because it means somebody copied the idea without the layout.|Never accept a UV photo from a seller as proof. Cameras shift the colour, the exposure flattens the response and a screenshot of a phone screen adds its own glow. Insist on a live video call with the torch in frame, or check it yourself on arrival.|On a passport, run the torch along the data page and the inside of the cover. On a licence, along the bottom edge and behind the portrait. On an identity card, across the whole face. Anywhere the response is absent or smeared, the document is a print.|UV is not conclusive on its own. It tells you whether the physical layer was built properly, not whether the number is real. Pair it with a number format check and, where possible, a register lookup.|Which torch should I buy?|Any three hundred and sixty five nanometre UV torch from a hardware shop. Under ten pounds and it lasts years.|Will a UV torch damage the document?|No. The ink is designed for repeated inspection and the wavelength is harmless.|What if the seller refuses a UV shot?|They either do not have the torch or do not want you to see the response. Both are reasons to hold the deposit back.
